# ABL Space Systems

> ABL Space Systems is an American launch vehicle company founded in 2017 by Harry O'Hanley and Dan Piemont, headquartered in El Segundo, California. ABL built RS1, a two-stage kerosene/liquid-oxygen small satellite launch vehicle rated at 1,350 kg to low Earth orbit and 1,000 kg to sun-synchronous orbit, powered by nine in-house additively manufactured E2 engines on the first stage and one on the second, together with GS0, a containerized ground system that packages an entire orbital launch site into standard shipping containers. RS1 flew once, from Pacific Spaceport Complex Kodiak in January 2023, and was lost shortly after liftoff; a second vehicle was destroyed during preflight static-fire testing in July 2024. The company exited the commercial orbital launch market in November 2024 and rebranded as Long Wall in February 2025, refocusing on containerized missile defense — the Cyclops exoatmospheric interceptor, RSX low-cost liquid boosters for flight testing and threat replication, and the Ironwood ground support equipment line.

## Products

- [RS1](https://ablspacesystems.com/rs1/): Small satellite launch vehicle — 1,350 kg to LEO, 1,000 kg to SSO, 9x E2 engines on stage one, 1x E2 on stage two, stages sized to standard shipping containers.
- [GS0](https://ablspacesystems.com/gs0/): Fully containerized ground/launch system deployable to any flat concrete pad by land, sea or air.
- Cyclops (Long Wall): Mass-producible exoatmospheric interceptor.
- RSX (Long Wall): Low-cost liquid boosters for flight testing and threat replication, derived from RS1.
- Ironwood (Long Wall): Modern ground support equipment with command-and-control, power and comms.
